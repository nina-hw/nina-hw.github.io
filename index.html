SYSTEM 01

Building Australia's First Lifecycle Engine

Can you build something from nothing, and know when to rebuild it once it stops working?

What I built

No CRM, no lead history, no attribution. Just personal relationships and direct mail, postcards and airmail. I built a database of the Australian target market and ran an email blast system, everyone received the same offer. Simple on purpose, no segmentation, no scoring, just consistent communication where none existed before.

Messaging thinking

I didn't write the emails alone. I worked with the Australian BDMs, they knew the market and the language better than I did. My job was the strategy underneath: which lifecycle stage needed education versus a commercial push, when to lead with a customer's actual problem instead of what we sold, and what signaled someone was ready for a sales conversation. BDMs wrote the words. I decided what the words needed to do.

What broke

Revenue growth stalled. Everyone was getting the same message, regardless of industry, job title, persona, or where they actually were in the buying process.

What I changed

Rebuilt around lifecycle, redefined our ICPs, and moved messaging into real collaboration with BDMs instead of one blanket offer. Set up a dedicated campaign for dormant clients, win-back, which had never existed before. Customers who'd gone quiet weren't treated like new leads, we acknowledged the relationship first and re-established relevance before making another offer.

Result

42% of Australia revenue, first-touch attributed to the original blast system. Win-back alone generated 66% of email-attributed opportunities.

What I learned

The system that got us to 42% wasn't the system that got us past it. Recognizing that mattered as much as building the original system.

SYSTEM 02

Teaching Sales What Matters First

Do you solve problems with logic first, or wait for the tool to solve it for you?

What I built

Inbound leads had no prioritization. BDMs spent time chasing volume, not fit. I defined a manual filtering system, no software, ICP fit and actual service offerings, non-trivial for a company that could offer many different technical skills to a client.

Messaging thinking

The score wasn't just "does this account match on paper." Someone who fit the ICP but only downloaded generic content wasn't scored the same as someone repeatedly engaging with content about a specific service. Fit told us who to consider. Behavior told us who was actually close.

Result

Qualified lead volume up 48%. MQL-to-SQL acceptance from 64% to 91%. Closed-won grew 5x.

What changed, years later

Same manual ICP logic, now running through RocketReach enrichment. Automation didn't improve the logic, the logic was already proven by hand. It removed the research hours behind it, faster handoff, faster follow-up.

What I learned

The logic came first, proven by hand. Automation followed once the volume earned it.

SYSTEM 03 · CURRENT BUILD

Building in Public

Can you do this today, with the current AI-native stack? This one's live and unfinished, not a closed case study.

What I'm building

Most CDPs are built for one buyer making one decision. B2B is a buying group, and the CDPs built for that are priced for companies much bigger than this one. So: Clay sources and enriches accounts against a defined ICP. Dynamics 365 stays the source of truth and calculates the score, ICP fit, external signals, first-party engagement. Above threshold, Sales gets it, and it has to be timely. Below threshold, it goes into nurture in Instantly.ai.
